    Methodology and glossary: choose the right source

    Different sources answer different questions. Start with your question, then check who and which period the table covers.

    Which data answer your question?
    Your questionStart withWhat it can tell you
    Who works here?Who is represented in the workforceTreasury Board counts and group shares. Core public administration tables cover a different population from tables for the whole federal public service.
    What do employees experience?Public Service Employee Survey (PSES)Respondents’ views on their workplace, including harassment, discrimination, respect and career opportunities. These percentages describe respondents to each question.
    What happens in hiring and promotion?Public Service Commission staffing dataApplications, appointments and employment movements. A group’s share of appointments is different from an individual’s chance of being appointed.
    Do employees report barriers in staffing?Staffing and Non-Partisanship Survey (SNPS)Respondents’ experiences of fairness, transparency and barriers. Some questions apply only to people who took part in a staffing process.
    What did leaders commit to?Call to Action and departmental plansReported commitments, goals and wording in plans. Read these beside workforce and survey results; a commitment or a word count does not establish an outcome.

    Terms used in the tables

    Representation
    The share of the stated workforce who self-identify as belonging to a group. Groups can overlap.
    Workforce availability
    An estimate of the potential labour pool for the relevant jobs and population. Use the benchmark published for that organization or series.
    Percentage points
    The difference between two percentages. A change from 10% to 12% is an increase of 2 percentage points.
    Not published or suppressed
    The source does not provide a usable value. Treat it as unavailable, never as zero, and do not reconstruct a suppressed count.
    Reporting population
    The people or positions included by the publisher; federal datasets do not all cover the same employers.
    Headcount
    The number of employees counted at a stated date or over a stated period; it is not necessarily full-time equivalents.
    Full-time equivalents (FTEs)
    An estimate of work performed in full-time positions over a period, not a count of distinct employees.
    Self-identification
    Employee information supplied through the applicable self-identification process; undeclared identity is not the same as membership outside a group.
    Respondent base
    The people whose answers contribute to a survey result; it may differ by question and group.
    Conditional survey question
    A question asked only of respondents who meet a condition, such as having experienced a staffing process.
    Weighted survey result
    A survey estimate adjusted using the publisher’s stated weighting method; it is not a headcount.
    Appointment share
    The fraction of appointments associated with a group; this is not an applicant’s probability of selection.
    Comparability
    Whether two measures use sufficiently consistent populations, definitions, questions and periods to support a comparison.
    Commitment versus outcome
    An intention or planned activity is not evidence that employee conditions changed; implementation and results need separate verification.
    Source edition
    A particular publication or data release; newer editions may change coverage or revise earlier figures.
    Organization boundary
    The specific employer or unit covered by a record; mergers, transfers and renamed branches can affect trends.

    Use public data with AI safely

    Follow your organization's approved tools and security rules. Public data may be suitable for an authorized AI tool, but no tool guarantees accuracy or privacy. Check every number against the original source.

    Use: published tables and policies. Never enter personal record identifiers, names linked to workplace cases, medical notes, internal correspondence, or protected/classified information in public AI tools.

    What employees reported about staffing

    The 2025 Staffing and Non-Partisanship Survey received 82,987 responses (30.9% response rate). Published question-level results: 45% said staffing processes reduce or eliminate biases; 15% reported being disadvantaged; 76% agreed selection is done fairly. The survey reports respondents’ experiences, not a finding about every employee or process.
