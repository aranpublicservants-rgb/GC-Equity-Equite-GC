Laws and regulations establish duties. Official guidance explains their application. Suggested examples, tools and implementation arrangements are identified as such.

Section 01

Understand the hazard

Racism and discrimination can create harmful conditions of work through conduct, decisions, policies and repeated failures to respond. Federal prevention frameworks cover psychological health as well as physical health. The Labour Program’s psychological health guidance explicitly identifies discrimination as a risk factor and connects psychological hazards to the hazard prevention program.

Sources : [6] [7]

Hazard
A source or condition with the potential to cause harm. Here, assess the harmful workplace exposure, such as repeated racist hostility or a discriminatory process.
Risk
The likelihood and seriousness of harm, considering who is exposed, how often, for how long and what protections exist.
Systemic discrimination
Disadvantage produced or maintained by organizational rules and practices. A rule that appears neutral can still have discriminatory effects. The Canadian Human Rights Act addresses discriminatory employment policies and practices.
Psychological injury or illness
A possible health outcome. A diagnosis is a separate clinical matter. A prevention assessment examines potential harm before an injury has to occur.

Sources : [1] [3] [11]

Describe the hazardous condition precisely. An employee’s race, religion, disability or identity is never the hazard. The assessment concerns the treatment, exposure or organizational practice that may cause harm.

Look beyond a single incident

A racial slur, religious harassment, repeated exclusion, disability-related humiliation or retaliation may require an individual response and a broader prevention review. Also examine decision rules, management discretion, complaint handling and the conditions that allow harmful conduct to recur. An observed difference between groups is a signal to examine, not a legal finding by itself.

Sources : [5] [11] [15]

Consider intersecting grounds. A Black woman with a disability may face an exposure that cannot be understood by looking at race, sex or disability separately. Section 3.1 of the Canadian Human Rights Act expressly addresses combined grounds.

Sources : [11]

Section 02

What the federal framework requires

Part II of the Canada Labour Code applies to the public service through section 240 of the Federal Public Sector Labour Relations Act, with adaptations. Confirm the employer, employment status and applicable regulations. A departmental policy example does not establish identical rules for every federal organization.

Sources : [2]

What follows from these duties

Racism-related exposures are not excluded because the legislation does not list every hazard by name. Official Labour Program guidance already connects discrimination and psychological hazards to prevention. When credible information points to a work-related risk, apply the relevant identification and assessment process. Whether a particular practice breaches a particular provision depends on the evidence and the applicable legal test.

Sources : [5] [6] [7]

Part XIX does not prescribe a separate racism-labelled form. Naming racism and discrimination in the policy, assessment methodology and hazard record is a recommended way to make the relevant exposures visible and assessable. The duty to assess and prevent covered risks comes from the legislation.

Current duties and possible future regulatory changes

The Labour Program’s 2026 consultation report discusses proposals for clearer psychological health and safety requirements. A consultation report does not enact those proposals. This resource relies on the existing Code, regulations and identified official guidance. Check the live consolidated legislation when making a decision.

Sources : [27]

In the harassment and violence regulations, the “applicable partner” is the policy committee or, if there is no policy committee, the workplace committee or health and safety representative. The regulations specify which matters must be done jointly.

Source : [4]

Section 03

Connect prevention, mental health and equity

A policy states the organization’s commitments. The hazard prevention program turns them into a repeatable process. The harassment and violence framework adds specific joint assessments and occurrence-resolution duties. Mental health planning should use this prevention work and measure its results.

How the instruments work together
InstrumentWhat to includeWhat the organization should be able to show
OHS policyExplicit recognition of racism, discrimination and psychological harm; reporting and accountability.A clear commitment, responsible roles and understandable reporting routes.
Hazard prevention programExposure descriptions, evidence, risk assessment, controls, training and effectiveness reviews.An updated hazard record and proof that the measures operate.
Harassment and violence assessment and policyOrganizational risk factors, prevention measures, accessible notices and required reviews.Joint assessment records and follow-up under the regulations.

The federal mental health strategy and the National Standard

The Federal Public Service Workplace Mental Health Strategy calls for organizational action plans, capacity and reporting on progress. The National Standard of Canada for Psychological Health and Safety in the Workplace offers a management-system approach to preventing psychological harm. The standard is voluntary; its status differs from the Code and regulations. Check the current edition through the Commission’s page.

Sources : [33] [34]

TBS’s 2026–27 Departmental Plan commits to helping organizations integrate the mental health strategy into their overall health and safety programs. That is a published commitment, not evidence that every organization has completed integration.

Source : [35]

A published federal example

The DND/CAF policy manual places harassment and violence assessments within its overall hazard prevention program. Annex C describes racist, sexist, homophobic and ableist behaviour as risk factors and discusses psychological harm. This is a concrete departmental example of integration. Its scope and procedures remain specific to the Defence Team.

Sources : [16]

The federal guide Building Success recommends joint governance and explains that adding psychological health to an existing prevention program can reduce duplication. The National Joint Council directive also lists psychological hazard sources, including bullying, harassment, stress and workplace mobbing.

Sources : [9] [10]

Recommended approach: use one coordinated prevention record, with appropriate access controls, to connect OHS, harassment prevention, anti-racism, accessibility and mental health work. Keep individual case files and confidential clinical information in their authorized systems.

Section 04

Mental health outcomes and the evidence

Systemic racism can affect access to opportunities, control over work, fair treatment and safety when raising concerns. Repeated exposure can become a continuing source of stress. The Public Health Agency of Canada identifies anti-Black racism and systemic discrimination as drivers of health inequities. International research associates reported racism with poorer mental health, including depression, anxiety and psychological stress.

Sources : [20] [21]

Read each figure within its population and measure
EvidencePublished resultWhat it can tell us
2024 Public Service Employee Survey, as reported by Health Canada43% of Black respondents reported that racism in their department or agency had negatively affected their mental health.Direct self-reported experience of racism-related mental health harm. This is not a clinical diagnosis or a count of all Black public servants.
2024 Public Service Employee Survey, as reported by Health Canada15% of Black respondents reported workplace discrimination, compared with 8% of non-racialized, non-Indigenous respondents.A difference in reported exposure. It is a different measure from the mental health question.
Statistics Canada, pooled 2021 to 2024 Canadian Social Survey31% of people aged 15 or older who experienced discrimination reported fair or poor mental health, compared with 14% of those who did not.A general population association across settings. These are not federal public service rates.

Sources : [17] [18] [19]

Use the source’s wording, population, period and response rules. Do not combine these percentages or treat respondents as the full workforce. The Statistics Canada comparison concerns discrimination in the five years before the survey, not only workplace racism.

What the research adds

Paradies and colleagues’ 2015 systematic review analysed 293 studies, predominantly from the United States. It found associations between reported racism and poorer mental health. Study designs and samples varied. The findings support prevention, but do not establish the cause of a particular person’s illness or the size of a Canadian departmental effect.

Sources : [21]

The United States study by Shannon and colleagues in 2009 followed workers across two survey waves. Racial harassment and discrimination helped explain occupational illness, injury or assault differences involving Hispanic respondents. Its findings must not be generalized into a Canadian injury rate or an identical effect for every racial group.

Sources : [22]

Connect the exposure to the outcome

A prevention model to examine with local evidence
Possible exposurePathway to examinePreventive response to test
Repeated racist hostility or exclusionContinuing threat, humiliation, isolation or stress.Stop the conduct, protect reporting and verify whether exposure recurs.
Opaque or inconsistently applied employment decisionsLow control, perceived injustice and fear of career harm.Use documented criteria, review decisions and correct discriminatory barriers.
Unresolved accommodation or religious-practice barriersContinued exposure to avoidable demands or exclusion.Put suitable interim measures in place and resolve the underlying process barrier.
Retaliation or disbelief after reportingFurther stress and reduced confidence in seeking help.Provide an alternative route, monitor for reprisal and follow up on safety.

This table is an assessment model, not a finding about a workplace. Health outcomes have multiple influences. A hazard assessment can record a credible risk pathway without demanding an employee’s diagnosis.

Measure health without medicalizing the employee

Track whether harmful conditions stop, whether employees feel safe reporting and whether unresolved barriers decrease. Use authorized aggregate health indicators cautiously. Leave, disability claims, EAP use and turnover cannot by themselves establish that racism caused the outcome. Low EAP use may also reflect access or trust barriers.

Offer culturally responsive and accessible support alongside preventive action. Health Canada’s Black-centric Employee Assistance Program (EAP) work includes identity-based matching and trauma-informed support. Availability depends on the employer’s EAP arrangements. Employees seeking care can ask their provider about their preferences and confidentiality.

Sources : [18]

Recommended practice: connect mental health reporting to the specific workplace conditions being changed. Record who is responsible for removing the exposure as well as who provides support.

Section 05

Identify and assess the exposure

The following practical method applies the prevention cycle to racism and discrimination. The six program components come from Part XIX. The questions and work products below are suggested implementation tools.

Six components of a working prevention program
ComponentQuestions to answerSuggested work product
1. Implementation planWho leads, who participates and when will each stage happen? What needs immediate protection?A dated plan with executive responsibility, resources and review points.
2. Identification methodologyWhich evidence will be used? How will workers participate safely? How will missing information be recorded?An agreed method with lawful data sources and clear decision rules.
3. Hazard identification and assessmentWhat condition can cause harm? Who is exposed, where, how often and for how long? Which controls already exist?A hazard record separating reported facts, analysis, uncertainty and decisions.
4. Preventive measuresCan the harmful practice be removed? What interim protection is needed? Could the measure create another risk?A control plan naming the owner, due date and test of effectiveness.
5. Employee trainingCan employees recognize exposures and use the reporting routes? Can managers intervene and assess systemic conditions?Role-specific learning with realistic examples and checks of understanding.
6. Program evaluationDid the exposure change? Did affected employees experience improvement? What must be revised?An effectiveness review with evidence, outstanding risks and next actions.

Sources : [3] [8]

Write a usable hazard description

Use a condition, an exposure and a possible harm. For example: “Staff in a public-facing unit report repeated racial hostility from clients. Procedures do not explain when employees can obtain immediate supervisor assistance. Continued exposure may cause psychological harm.” This is an illustrative description, not an account of a specific workplace.

Assess severity and exposure separately

  • Consider the possible seriousness of harm, including a single severe exposure.
  • Examine repetition, duration, power imbalance and the ability to leave or challenge the condition.
  • Check whether protections work for the employees most exposed, including intersecting identities.
  • Record uncertainty and missing information. Limited reporting does not establish low risk.

A numerical matrix may help set priorities, but the organization must define its scale. A low frequency score must not obscure potentially serious harm. A score does not decide whether discrimination occurred.

Document why an exposure was included, deferred or excluded and who reviewed the decision. A requirement to prove discriminatory intent, obtain a diagnosis or win a complaint before examining risk would undermine a preventive assessment.

Section 06

Use evidence safely and together

A useful assessment combines measures of employee experience with evidence about workplace processes. Check whether sources point to a common exposure, where they disagree and whose experience is missing. Keep each source’s population and period visible.

Evidence that can inform prevention
SourceWhat to examineLimit or safeguard
Published employee surveysRacism-related mental health impact, discrimination, harassment, reporting confidence and reasons for not reporting.Verify the question number and wording for each year. Respect suppression and response rules.
Authorized process reviewsAccommodation delays, documented staffing criteria, performance-review practices, complaint handling and inconsistent decisions.Use records only for an authorized purpose. Avoid assigning protected-group identity by inference.
Harassment and violence recordsRisk factors, recurrence, process barriers and preventive recommendations.Keep identities and individual resolution material out of general dashboards.
Ombuds and independent feedback trendsRecurring conditions and barriers described through confidential channels.Use only trends that can lawfully and safely be shared under the office’s mandate. No disclosure of case files.
Employee networks and listening sessionsPatterns that administrative measures miss and whether proposed measures would help.Participation should be voluntary, accessible and safe. Explain limits of anonymity and report back.
Authorized aggregate health and work indicatorsAbsence, turnover or disability patterns that may warrant further examination.Indicators have multiple causes. Do not infer diagnoses, identities or racism-attributable costs.

Follow a consistent analysis method

  1. Define the work unit, reporting period, exposure and question being examined.
  2. List what each source measures, its coverage and its limitations.
  3. Compare independent signals and test plausible alternative explanations.
  4. Record the preventive decision, missing information and a date to revisit it.

Several signals can strengthen a concern without proving discrimination. One credible report can also warrant assessment and immediate protection. Requiring multiple matching datasets in every case would miss serious or underreported exposures.

What public service reports contribute

The Auditor General’s 2023 audit of six organizations found gaps in using evidence and measuring equity and inclusion outcomes. It supports asking whether action changes employee experience, rather than counting activities alone.

Sources : [23]

Many Voices One Mind documents Indigenous employee barriers and recommends institutional changes. The DND leadership message on Rachel Zellars’ study describes accounts from Black executives of career barriers and emotional, psychological and physical impacts, including on families. These accounts help identify questions and mechanisms to assess. They do not measure prevalence across the whole public service.

Sources : [24] [25]

Respect purpose limits and privacy

The Privacy Act limits federal collection, use and disclosure of personal information. Subsection 9(3) of the Employment Equity Act restricts the use of workforce information collected under paragraph 9(1)(a) to obligations under that Act. Do not repurpose those confidential self-identification records for a separate hazard dataset.

Sources : [12] [13]

Use published, appropriately aggregated evidence and separately authorized sources. A Gender-based Analysis Plus approach can help frame questions about different experiences and intersecting barriers; it does not create authority to collect or link personal information.

  • Set the purpose, legal authority, access rules and retention before collecting data.
  • Use the least identifying detail needed. Apply suppression, including where combined tables could reveal a small group.
  • Keep medical information, identifiable case records and general prevention reporting separate.
  • Never reconstruct suppressed cells or identify people through small-group comparisons.

Section 07

Choose measures that change conditions

Part XIX sets a priority order for preventive measures: eliminate the hazard, reduce it, provide protective equipment where relevant, then use administrative procedures. Apply that hierarchy thoughtfully to psychological exposures. Protective equipment will not correct a discriminatory decision rule. Support and training have a role, but the control plan must address the source of the assessed exposure.

Sources : [3]

The examples below are suggested measures for assessment and adaptation. They are not findings about a department or prescribed solutions for every situation.

Examples of exposures, controls and effectiveness checks
Exposure to assessSuggested measureCheck whether it works
Racist comments, degrading jokes or exclusion are tolerated.Intervene promptly; stop the conduct; clarify expected behaviour; apply fair accountability and an alternative reporting route.Ask whether exposure recurs and whether employees can report without adverse treatment.
Staffing or performance decisions rely on unexplained discretion.Use documented criteria and reasons, structured review and a way to challenge inconsistent application.Review sampled decisions and whether identified barriers or inconsistencies are corrected.
Disability-related barriers persist during accommodation delays.Provide appropriate interim measures; identify the decision-maker; set response standards; limit medical information to authorized needs.Track time to an effective measure and employee confirmation that the work barrier changed.
Religious observance is handled inconsistently or attracts hostility.Review scheduling and accommodation rules; document reasons; address hostile treatment.Check access to accommodations and whether the same rule is applied consistently and lawfully.
Public-facing employees repeatedly face racist client abuse.Set escalation procedures, immediate supervisor support and service arrangements that reduce exposure.Check whether employees receive timely help and whether exposure frequency decreases.
People report career harm after raising concerns.Create a route outside the implicated reporting line; document protective measures; review subsequent adverse decisions fairly.Follow up with consent and examine new barriers or adverse treatment.
Restructuring adds unequal workloads or unclear selection decisions.Assess change-related exposures before implementation where possible; document criteria and review disproportionate effects.Check workloads, decision consistency and new reports of harm after the change.

Check the measure before approving it

Ask whether it removes the harmful condition, shifts the burden onto the affected employee, exposes an identity, reduces career opportunities or creates another hazard. Consult affected employees safely. An involuntary move that disadvantages the reporting employee may create further harm. Record alternatives and reasons.

Make training practical

Use role-specific scenarios on recognizing discriminatory exposures, receiving reports, fair decisions, accommodation and reprisal. Check whether managers can apply the process. The harassment and violence regulations require training to address the relationship with the Canadian Human Rights Act’s prohibited grounds.

Section 08

Responsibilities and employee network participation

The employer remains responsible for its statutory duties. OHS, harassment prevention, mental health, human resources and equity functions need coordinated responsibilities. Employee expertise should inform decisions without transferring the employer’s responsibilities to volunteers.

Sources : [1] [3] [4] [9] [31]

Do employee networks have a statutory committee seat?

The Code sets membership rules, including selection of employee representatives. It does not create an automatic seat for an employee network. Section 135.1 should be read with the employer’s applicable arrangements.

Sources : [31]

Recommended participation routes include invited expertise, structured consultation, written submissions and an advisory working group reporting into the statutory process. Agree these arrangements with the relevant parties. Provide accessible participation time and return a written account of the decision and follow-up. A lack of a permanent seat should not prevent the organization from receiving relevant evidence.

Keep unresolved concerns visible

Maintain the full issue list, including deferred measures and reasons. Champions can raise unresolved barriers with the responsible functional authority and senior leadership. Statutory participation and decisions must still follow the applicable legal framework. Independent feedback should remain able to reach the deputy head under its mandate.

Section 09

A practical implementation plan

Suggested practice This 90-day plan is a starting model, not a statutory grace period. Respond to urgent risks immediately and meet the applicable legal timelines.

Suggested first 90 days
TimingActionResult to bring back
First 10 daysName an executive sponsor and OHS lead. Identify immediate exposures, protective measures, the statutory partner and the consultation approach.Named responsibilities, interim protection and a clear place to report a concern.
By day 30Agree the assessment method and lawful evidence sources. Invite network and bargaining-agent input. Review policy language and known control gaps.A documented method, an initial issue list and reasons for priority decisions.
By day 60Complete priority assessments. Test measures with affected employees and implement measures within the responsible function’s authority.Hazard records, owners, due dates and evidence that measures operate.
By day 90Review effectiveness and unresolved risks with the statutory partner and senior leadership. Report back to employees in an accessible form.An action record showing completed work, outstanding risks, decisions and next review dates.

Urgent actionable improvements

These suggested changes can use existing processes. They require authority and follow-through even when the direct cost is limited.

  • Add explicit racism and discrimination prompts to the assessment method and relevant policy review.
  • Publish one clear directory of the OHS contact, designated recipient, representative and available support routes.
  • Add an owner, due date and effectiveness check to every accepted preventive action.
  • Put unresolved systemic exposures on the policy committee’s prevention agenda and the senior-management action record.
  • Review one recurring process barrier, such as unexplained decision criteria or an accommodation delay, and test a concrete correction.

Preserve a complete decision record

For each issue, record the exposure, evidence, possible harm, people or work affected, existing controls, decision, responsible executive, action owner, due date and review result. Record deferred actions and reasons. If a functional owner cannot act, identify the decision needed from the next level of authority.

Under resource constraints, prioritize the most serious and poorly controlled exposures, use existing joint governance and remove duplicated reporting. A resource constraint should be documented with the residual risk and escalation decision. It should not quietly remove a known concern from the issue list.

Review outcomes and respect legal timelines

Completion and effectiveness answer different questions. A course delivered, a policy signed or a committee meeting held shows an activity. A reduction in harmful exposure and greater reporting safety provide evidence about results.

These selected timelines do not replace the full requirements. A three-year cycle does not permit postponing a review triggered earlier by changed conditions or new information.

A quarterly management review

Suggested practice Review patterns, action status, residual risk, employee feedback and the next decisions each quarter. This proposed trend-review cadence is separate from the Code’s requirement that the policy committee meet at least quarterly. Escalate urgent issues sooner.

Sources : [31]

Connect material unresolved workforce risks to the organization’s corporate risk and planning processes. The hazard record should remain operational even when the same issue appears in a corporate risk profile, accessibility plan or departmental plan.

If you are experiencing harm at work

You can seek support and ask for preventive action while considering your formal options. You do not have to decide which legal label best describes everything before asking where to report an unsafe condition.

A workplace exposure needs to stopReport the condition through the employer’s OHS route. Describe what happens, where, its recurrence and the protection requested. Keep a dated record.Ask who will assess the concern, what interim protection is available and when you will hear back.
An occurrence of harassment or violence needs a responseGive notice to the employer or designated recipient identified in the prevention policy. A union representative or support person can help.The regulations provide a dedicated resolution process. A committee does not investigate an individual occurrence through the ordinary joint complaint route.

Prepare a short, factual request

The following is suggested wording for adaptation:

I am reporting a work-related exposure that may affect health and safety. The condition is repeated discriminatory treatment in a work process. Please identify the person responsible for assessing the concern, any interim protection available, the prevention process that will be used and the date for a written update. I can provide relevant examples through an appropriate confidential channel.

Describe events and effects accurately. Keep relevant messages and dates in an authorized place. Avoid including other people’s medical information or circulating confidential case material.

Keep the processes distinct

Hazard prevention can address conditions affecting several people. A harassment or human rights process examines its own issues under its own rules. Labour Program guidance says that concurrent proceedings can be possible. Get advice on your circumstances and preserve deadlines; there is no universal requirement to finish every informal step before seeking formal advice.

Sources : [5]

A refusal of dangerous work has a separate legal test and procedure under section 128. A discrimination concern does not automatically meet that test. Obtain prompt advice about the specific circumstances and follow the applicable process.

Section 13

Common questions

Does the hazard prevention program cover psychological hazards?

Labour Program guidance explicitly connects psychological hazards to Part XIX and identifies discrimination as a risk factor. The Code’s purpose includes preventing psychological injuries and illnesses. Describe the exposure and apply the relevant assessment process.

Sources : [1] [6] [7]

Must someone first win a discrimination complaint?

A prevention assessment asks about possible work-related harm. It is different from an adjudicator deciding whether a discriminatory practice has been established. Credible risk information should be assessed through the applicable process without requiring a completed individual case.

Sources : [3] [4] [5]

Does every disagreement become a hazard or discrimination?

No. Examine the actual conduct or condition, the work connection and the potential harm. Discrimination has its own legal tests. Legitimate management action must still be carried out fairly. A conclusion requires facts and reasons, not a label alone.

Sources : [11] [15]

Can systemic practices be assessed without an identified wrongdoer?

Yes. The assessment can examine a rule, process or organizational condition and its possible effects. The harassment and violence regulations require consideration of workplace culture, conditions and structure. Record what is known and what still needs testing.

Sources : [3] [4]

Does an EAP referral resolve the hazard?

Access to care can help an employee. The prevention review must also establish what happens to the harmful exposure. Record the action on working conditions and assess whether it works. EAP use is not proof that the organizational source has been controlled.

Sources : [7] [9]

What if the concern involves the function that receives reports?

Use the alternative reporting arrangements in the employer’s policy and ask about protections and independent advice. For a harassment or violence notice, identify the employer or designated recipient. An ombuds or union may help explain options within its mandate. Preserve formal deadlines while seeking support.

Sources : [4] [15]

Do fewer complaints prove that the workplace is safer?

No. Reporting can change with trust, access, fear and knowledge of the process. Read complaint counts alongside employee experience, recurrence and evidence about controls. Give the denominator and reporting period whenever a rate is published.

Sources : [17] [23]
